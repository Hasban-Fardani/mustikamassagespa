# Mustika Massage Spa — Image Art Direction

_Research date: 17 August 2026_

## Decision

The client reference is **space-first luxury hospitality**: warm amber light, cream linen, dark wood, brass/gold highlights, greenery, candlelight, and enough depth to make the treatment room feel inviting. The image is rich because the room, light, and tactile materials carry the story—not because decorative graphics have been added on top.

The current hero image points in the opposite direction. Its cool daylight, blue glass reflection, white uniform, visible jewellery, and tight hand/foot framing read as a modern reflexology clinic or beauty salon. It does not establish a premium spa environment and is too context-poor to lead a full-screen hero.

The replacement system should therefore use:

- an **environmental spa interior** for the first hero frame;
- a **professional treatment scene** for the second frame;
- an **aromatherapy/tactile detail** for the third frame;
- another **warm room or shared treatment scene** for the final frame;
- warm-neutral grading across the set, without forcing every photo into an orange filter.

Do not label a stock interior as “Ruang Mustika” or imply that a stock model is a Mustika therapist/customer. Until the client supplies an on-location shoot, use neutral labels such as “Suasana relaksasi”, “Ritual perawatan”, or no image caption at all.

## Recommended production set

For one coherent first pass, use this sequence without duplicating the same image in adjacent sections:

1. **Hero 01 — the place:** A1, wide and full-bleed.
2. **Hero 02 — the care:** B1, portrait image anchored to one side.
3. **Hero 03 — the ritual:** C1, tactile detail with room context.
4. **Hero 04 — together:** B4, wide shared-treatment scene.
5. **Service thumbnails:** B2 for targeted/reflex care, C2 for aromatherapy, B3 for full-body massage, and A3 for wellness/space.
6. **Final CTA:** A2, with restrained darkening for text contrast.

This order follows the client reference’s narrative: **enter the space → see professional care → notice the sensory details → imagine booking**.

## Reject criteria

Reject a candidate even if it is technically “spa” imagery when it contains any of the following:

- cool blue/white clinical lighting as the dominant colour;
- glass-office or salon context with no hospitality cues;
- hands, feet, or bare skin with no visible room or linen context;
- unrelated bathrooms, bathtubs, hotel bedrooms, or skincare products;
- heavy jewellery, casual street styling, visible branding, or medical equipment;
- 3D renders, impossible architecture, over-smoothed skin, malformed hands, or other synthetic-looking artefacts;
- a composition that requires an extreme crop to fit the intended slot.

## Licence and truthfulness notes

Pexels states that its photos can be used for free, modified, and used on websites; attribution is optional. It also prohibits implying that identifiable people endorse a product or service. ([Official Pexels licence](https://www.pexels.com/license/))

Before implementation:

1. open the chosen photo page and download from that page, not from a search-engine cache;
2. record the photographer, photo-page URL, download date, and local filename in the asset ledger;
3. inspect the original at 100% for artefacts and confirm the crop on desktop and mobile;
4. never describe stock models as Mustika staff or stock interiors as the actual Bandung venue;
5. replace the stock hero with a real Mustika location shoot when the client can provide one.
